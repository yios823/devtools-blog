About

What this site is

Short, builder-to-builder notes on running engineering teams, operating as a founder, automating the boring parts of dev work, and keeping infra in shape.

Each article tries to do one thing: hand you something you can use the same day. A runbook template you can paste into your wiki. A postmortem checklist for the next time prod breaks. A workflow that deletes a slow Monday.

Who writes it

A working engineer who has been on both sides of the on-call pager, written the postmortems nobody reads, and shipped enough small tools to know which ones save real time. Most pieces here come from notes written down right after a thing happened, edited later when there was time to think.

Why the simple design

Loud sites distract. The point is the words on the page. Black on white, system font, no JavaScript on most pages, no popups, no tracking pixels, no newsletter modal. If the page is good, you’ll come back. If not, you won’t, and that is fine.

What you’ll find

The site is organised into pillars. Each pillar covers a thread that takes a year of writing to do properly:

  • Engineering incident management and postmortems. How to write postmortems that change things. Runbook templates. SLA/SLO basics for small teams. Common incident patterns and what they reveal.
  • Founder operating systems and workflows. Notion setups for founders, weekly review systems, OKR tracking, investor CRM workflows. The unglamorous habits that keep things moving.
  • Automation with n8n and AI prompt engineering. Workflow recipes, prompt patterns for engineering tasks, where AI helps and where it bites.
  • MCP and AI tooling for DevOps. What MCP is, where it earns its keep, building servers that survive contact with real infrastructure.

Some articles touch tools I’ve packaged as paid products. Those links are tagged as such. The articles work without buying anything.

How to reach me

If a piece is wrong or could be sharper, message me. I read everything. The faster path is short and specific: “this paragraph is wrong because X” beats “great post.”
